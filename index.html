    Idaho is a state in the northwestern United States. Its capital is Boise, and it joined the Union on July 3, 1890, as the 43rd state.[1][8] Known as the Gem State, Idaho takes its nickname from the variety of gemstones found within its borders.[2]

    Idaho borders six U.S. states and the Canadian province of British Columbia. Its landscapes include mountain ranges, river canyons, and the volcanic Snake River Plain.[4][5] The U.S. Census Bureau estimated its population at 2,029,733 on July 1, 2025.[3]

    Agriculture and ranching sit alongside manufacturing, food processing, technology, energy, and tourism in the state’s economy.[11][12]

    Geography

    Idaho shares borders with Washington and Oregon to the west, Nevada and Utah to the south, and Montana and Wyoming to the east. British Columbia lies to the north. Portions of the western boundary follow the Snake River, while mountain divides shape much of the boundary with Montana.[4]

    The Snake River Plain extends roughly 400 miles across the state. Its eastern portion preserves a record of volcanic activity associated with the Yellowstone hotspot’s track; Craters of the Moon contains younger lava fields within this landscape.[5] Idaho.gov lists Mount Borah as the state’s highest point at 12,662 feet.[1]

    History

    Indigenous peoples

    Idaho’s history begins long before territorial boundaries and statehood. Five federally recognized tribes retain ancestral homelands in the region: the Coeur d’Alene Tribe, Kootenai Tribe of Idaho, Nez Perce Tribe, Shoshone-Bannock Tribes, and Shoshone-Paiute Tribes. The Shoshone-Paiute homeland and reservation cross the present Idaho–Nevada border. These are living communities with distinct histories and governments.[6]

    Territory and statehood

    Lewis and Clark crossed into the region at Lemhi Pass in 1805. Fur trading, missionary activity, and migration through the region followed. President Abraham Lincoln signed the act creating Idaho Territory on March 4, 1863.[7] Idaho became a state on July 3, 1890.[1][8]

    Population

    The 2020 census counted 1,839,106 Idaho residents. The Census Bureau’s July 1, 2025 estimate was 2,029,733, a 10.4% increase from its adjusted April 1, 2020 estimates base. That base differs slightly from the original census count.[3]

    Government

    Idaho’s state government has three branches: executive, legislative, and judicial. The governor leads the executive branch; the legislature includes a Senate and a House of Representatives; and the judiciary administers the state court system.[9]

    As verified on September 25, 2026, the governor is Brad Little. USAGov’s Idaho directory connects residents to state agencies, elected officials, local governments, and services such as education, elections, motor vehicles, and health and welfare.[10] Official information is available through Idaho.gov.

    Economy and natural resources

    Idaho Commerce identifies food processing, manufacturing, semiconductors, technology, aerospace, energy, outdoor recreation, and tourism among the state’s key industry sectors. This mix connects agricultural production with industrial and technical activity.[11]

    Agriculture, ranching, forests, and water are central to Idaho’s natural-resource economy. State programs address food safety, soil and water conservation, forest management, environmental quality, and energy and mineral resources. Idaho.gov’s natural-resources directory brings these agencies and programs together.[12]

    Education

    Idaho’s education system includes public schools, colleges and universities, community colleges, and career and technical programs. The Department of Education supports K–12 education, while the State Board of Education coordinates statewide education policy, including higher education.[13]

    State resources include school data, college directories, scholarships, financial aid, and career planning. The Idaho.gov education directory provides a starting point for students and families.[13]

    Parks and recreation

    Idaho’s outdoor destinations include Hells Canyon, Shoshone Falls, Sun Valley, and Craters of the Moon. River recreation, skiing, wildlife observation, and camping are among the activities highlighted by the state’s official website.[2]

    Craters of the Moon offers an example of the state’s volcanic landscape, with lava flows and volcanic features on the Snake River Plain.[5] The Idaho Department of Parks and Recreation and Visit Idaho provide park and visitor information.

    State symbols

    Idaho’s official symbols reflect its plants, wildlife, and mineral resources. Its flag, adopted in 1907, places the state seal on a blue field above a banner bearing the state’s name.[1]

    International connections

    The Department of State’s 2017–2021 archived Idaho profile describes international exchange programs, university partnerships, salmon-resource cooperation with Canada, and export activity. It reports approximately $4 billion in goods exports in 2018 and 86,038 passports issued in fiscal year 2018. These are historical figures from the archive, not present-day totals.[14]
